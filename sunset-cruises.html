Florida sunset cruises — the state's most popular activity

A Florida sunset cruise is a tradition. Most operators sail 75 minutes before sunset and return 30 minutes after, giving you a 90-minute to 2.5-hour window of golden-hour light, calm water, and (usually) dolphin sightings. Key West is the spiritual home — the iconic Western Union schooner, Mallory Square's nightly celebration, the Fury Catamaran, Sebago, and Wind & Wine all sell out 3-7 days ahead in winter season.

Beyond Key West, top sunset hubs include Naples Pier (legendary western horizon), Clearwater Beach (powdery sand + crystal water), Tampa Bay, Marathon, Destin (Emerald Coast), St. Pete Beach, and the Cocoa Beach pier. Most sails include complimentary wine and beer (sometimes champagne), light hors d'oeuvres, and live music or curated playlists. Some operators specialize: BYOB sails are the cheapest option; "wine and cheese" sails skew adult; family-friendly variants include juice for kids.

What to wear: layered casual — Florida sea breezes pick up after sundown even in summer. Bring a light jacket. Most sails depart from public marinas with paid parking ($10-$20). Book 5-10 days ahead in peak season (Dec-April); 24-48 hours is usually fine in summer.

What to expect

Most sunset cruises are 1.5 to 3 hours, departing 60–90 minutes before sunset. Drinks (open bar or BYOB), light snacks or canapés, and live music are common. Sail-only boats are more peaceful; party catamarans are louder with dancing. Group sizes range from 6 (private) to 80 (party boats). Check the booking flow for departure time on the day you plan to sail.
