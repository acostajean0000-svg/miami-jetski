Florida snorkel tours — the only living reef in the continental US

Florida's snorkel scene is anchored by the Florida Reef Tract, a 360-mile barrier reef running from Miami to the Dry Tortugas — the only living coral reef in the continental United States. Top reef destinations include John Pennekamp State Park (Key Largo), Sombrero Reef (Marathon), Sand Key and Western Sambo (Key West), Phil Foster Park / Blue Heron Bridge (Palm Beach), and the Sea Experience reef tours from Fort Lauderdale.

Off the reef, Florida's freshwater springs offer a completely different experience: Crystal River (November-March) is the only legal place in the US to swim with wild West Indian manatees, and Silver Springs + Rainbow Springs offer year-round clear-kayak and snorkel tours with rhesus monkeys, alligator gar, and ancient cypress.

What to bring: reef-safe sunscreen (mandatory by state law — no oxybenzone, octinoxate), swimwear, and a beach towel. Most operators provide masks, snorkels, fins, flotation noodles, and a 15-minute briefing. Trips run 2-6 hours; full-day charters often visit two reef sites and include lunch. Best visibility months: May-September.

What you should know

Most snorkel tours include gear (mask, snorkel, fins) and a guide. Reef trips run 3–6 hours; expect 2–3 stops. Scuba dives require certification (Open Water minimum); discover-scuba intros are available for beginners. Bring reef-safe sunscreen — many destinations now ban oxybenzone/octinoxate to protect coral.
